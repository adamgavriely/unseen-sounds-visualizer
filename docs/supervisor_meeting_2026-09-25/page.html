Pictures: Adam's complaint, and what it led to

Adam: the pictures showed the family tag ("vehicle", a generic siren) instead of the sound heard, and ignored the scene (a car door in a car drawn as a house door; a rooster drawn as a generic bird).

  • Generator matters most: across seeds, FLUX pictures were judged readable ~30 % of the time, Qwen-Image ~48 % one rater, name shown.
  • V3 draws the most specific sound the detector heard (its own sub-labels, a tie → the common parent). Reading its subjects before drawing showed it invented things the audio never established ("a person swinging a sword" for a whoosh; "a police car" for a bare siren).
  • V3.1 lets the scene add a qualifier only ("car door", "farm vehicle"), never a new noun or a person; a word-list from the AudioSet ontology refuses kinds the audio did not establish. No model is asked to approve its own answer.
  • Side effect: V3 changes what the gate shows on 2 of 49 DEV clips (a generic "vehicle" picture no longer merges with the train it duplicates), about +0.08 wrong pictures per clip. Reported as a cost, not tuned away.
  • Measured blind: Adam types what each picture shows, with no name under it; repeats measure his own consistency; the answer sheet was committed before any answer. V3 was not dropped after its subjects were read: it is rated as registered. V3.1 is tested on slice B (30 clips, topped up if too few sounds), with a second pass (name + scene frame: "anything false?") — a rule added this week.

The automatic judge, rebuilt

Before: one model described the picture, and that model also wrote the picture's subject — circular. The reference sentence was written from the detector's own output — circular again. Now: Gemma-4-31B looks at the pictures directly, against a reference built only from the annotator's labels. It must pass two trust checks on 139 clips before its ranking is shown (does it track the annotator's cost; does it notice a known-wrong picture).

Captions are shown to the judge as the words themselves, so it never has to recognise anything; pictures must be recognised from pixels. That favours captions.

What we do not claim

  • That the gate beats "draw everything" on F1. It does not; it wins on precision and viewer cost.
  • That pictures beat silence on viewer cost overall. On the 13 off-screen TEST clips silence costs a viewer 3.08 more per clip [+1.08, +5.38] (23 Sept look); on all 60 it is a tie.
  • Any picture-quality number from more than one rater. Every gold number is one annotator.
  • The timing gain on TEST.
