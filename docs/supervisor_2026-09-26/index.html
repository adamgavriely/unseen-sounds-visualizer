Seeing off-screen sound

A deaf viewer misses sounds that are heard but not seen. Our system finds those sounds and shows a small picture of them beside the video. No model was trained; existing models are chained.

In one line: against existing approaches (a strong sound detector + a picture for every sound it finds), ours wins clearly: F1 +0.25, significant (section 4). Against our own pipeline without gate, F1 is a tie, but the gate shows half as many wrong pictures, and the real sounds it loses are too few to measure. Sections 3–6 show why.

The gate does what it should: it removes pictures of things already on screen. It also silences a few real sounds, and F1 counts those losses as heavily as the gains, so F1 ties (section 6).

How much should a wrong picture weigh? (β, checked after the fact)

β is the price of one wrong picture; a missed sound always costs 4. Example: β = 1 means four wrong pictures hurt as much as one missed sound. Here “clearly better” means the 95 % interval is below zero, not only the middle value.

  • TEST (60 clips): ours is clearly better than show nothing for β ≤ 1.1, and clearly better than the pipeline without gate for β ≥ 1.2. No single β wins both clearly on TEST alone.
  • DEV + TEST (109 clips): ours is clearly better than both at once for β from 0.9 to 1.5.

This band was computed after the results (post hoc), so it is a description, not a new test. It tells the reader for which prices of a wrong picture the gate is worth it.

The gate removes pictures of sources the viewer can already see, so it can only help where such pictures exist. On off-screen clips there is nothing to remove, so the win over “show nothing” there comes from the detector and the pictures, not from the gate. 6 · Why sounds are missed (F1 does not move)

Most misses happen before the gate: no detector ever hears them

Left: every missed needed sound in the frozen-table runs, with a reason for each. 28 of 49 (57 %) were never detected by any of our detectors (orange). The gate wrongly said “visible” for only 3 on DEV and 3 on TEST. Right: an object detector is at chance at the “is the source visible?” question; the large vision-language model is better.

When we give the system the human's sound list instead of the detector's, the gate's F1 gain becomes significant (+0.067). So the ceiling is the sound detector — today's best models still miss sounds under speech and music. “Timing” means a picture came, but too late or too early to count (section 9).

New: every picture is checked, and redrawn if wrong (after the frozen table)

How it works

  • The vision-language model (Qwen3.8-27B) gets a multiple-choice question: “What is the main thing in this picture?” The options are shuffled. Example: “A. a fire alarm bell · B. a desk bell · C. a kettle …”.
  • A text check (OCR, reading any letters in the picture) catches pictures with wrong words in them.
  • If the answer is wrong, it draws again — up to 4 tries. Then it shows a word card instead.

Checked before we used it

  • Caught 7 / 7 known bad pictures and 33 / 33 deliberately wrong pictures.
  • Wrongly rejected 3 of 68 good pictures (4.4 %).
  • Gave the same answer after reshuffling the options in 113 / 115 cases.

Examples it fixed

  • Fire alarm drawn as a desk bell → a red wall bell.
  • Crowd drawn as crows → people.
  • Steam drawn as a kettle → a pipe valve.
  • Typing drawn as a shouting man → hands on a keyboard.
  • One car horn could not be drawn well → a “HONK” word card.

The picture is what the viewer actually reads, so it had to be tested by a person, blind. The earlier automatic picture scorers (asked “how good is this picture?”) disagreed with the human, so we do not use them to score. The new check is different: it only asks “is this the right object?” as a pass/fail guard, and it was tested on pictures with a known answer before use. It is not used for any score.

9 · Timing and display

Pictures used to appear before the sound — up to 9 seconds early

Watch when the picture appears compared with the shots: before the fix it jumps in early; after the fix it starts with the sound.

before223 of 442 picture starts were moved too early
afterA picture may never start before its sound

Found by tracing each step, not by tuning. It improved DEV significantly; on TEST the change was too small to tell, and we report exactly that.

Display rules today (after the frozen table)

  • A picture stays at most 1 second after its sound ends.
  • If the same sound repeats within 1.5 seconds, the repeats become one picture. Example: a dog barks three times in 2 seconds → one dog picture, not three flashes.
  • No fading: pictures show at full strength (before, they faded to 77 %).

A scoring check: what if we are less strict about timing? (report only)

The main rule counts a picture as right only if it starts near the start of its sound. Two looser readings, shown only to see how much timing matters. Viewer cost per clip, lower is better; the main rule stays the one we report.

11 · Honest limits (so you hear them from me first)

Seven things an examiner will ask about

  • Helpfulness for deaf viewers is not tested. All results are about sounds and pictures, scored against labels. No deaf viewer used the system. This goes in the abstract.
  • One annotator labelled all 139 clips. A second person on 30 clips is planned, to report agreement. This also goes in the abstract.
  • TEST was looked at more than once (5 planned reads; 11 logged exposures counting printouts and, on 28 Sept, the results inspector), all dated and disclosed. The final table was scored once, under a rule written before. The two existing-approach baselines were each scored once on TEST, with their threshold chosen on DEV. Nothing was tuned on TEST.
  • The detector threshold was chosen on clips that overlap TEST (35 of 60). Both systems use it; a clean check on DEV picked the same value.
  • Things changed after the frozen table. The frozen TEST table does not include them, and it stays as it is:
    – confidence floor 0.40: chosen on DEV, passed on the 415 held-out AudioSet clips;
    – BEATs self-check instead of PANNs: tested on the 415 held-out clips (the frozen table used PANNs);
    – family-rule bug fix: simulated on the scored DEV and TEST renders; found while inspecting a TEST clip (a logic fix, not a tuned setting);
    – Qwen-Image pictures, the picture check and the display rules: they change what the viewer sees, not the per-sound scores. Pictures were tested by a blind human rating on new clips; the check was validated on pictures with known answers.
  • The human picture test has one rater (me) (round 2, blind). The final sealed sitting was cancelled on 28 Sept; the validated picture check replaces it. That check is a model check, not a human test.
  • Viewer cost was introduced after F1 came out a tie. Its weights date from before; we show results for every weight, not one chosen point.
