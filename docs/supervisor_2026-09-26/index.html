MSc project · Adam Gavriely · Bar-Ilan · 27 Sept 2026

Seeing off-screen sound

A deaf viewer misses sounds that are heard but not seen. Our system finds those sounds and shows a small picture of them beside the video. No model was trained; existing models are chained.

In one line: F1, the main number I registered, is a tie. But the gate shows half as many wrong pictures as drawing every sound, and the real sounds it loses are too few to measure. Sections 3–5 show why.

1 · The idea

Draw a sound only when the viewer cannot see where it comes from

Press play on both. Left: a siren is heard but no ambulance is on screen → a picture appears on the right. Right: the waterfall is on screen → nothing is drawn.

Heard, not seen → drawnLeft half: the video. Right half: the panel the deaf viewer reads.
Heard and seen → not drawnA picture of a visible waterfall adds nothing.

This “gate” is the project's main idea: fewer pictures, but only useful ones.

The gate does what it should: it removes pictures of things already on screen. It also silences a few real sounds, and F1 counts those losses as heavily as the gains, so F1 ties (section 5).

The gate removes pictures of sources the viewer can already see, so it can only help where such pictures exist. On off-screen clips there is nothing to remove, so the win over “show nothing” there comes from the detector and the pictures, not from the gate. On “mixed” clips “show nothing” (5.33) is still cheaper than ours — an honest weak spot. Ours beats “show nothing” overall only if a wrong picture costs less than 2.56 points (TEST) / 2.33 (DEV). 5 · Why F1 does not move

Most misses happen before the gate: the sound detector never hears them

Left: of 21 missed sounds, 11 (orange) were never heard by the detector; only 3 were wrongly judged “visible”. Right: an object detector is at chance at the “is the source visible?” question; the large vision-language model is better.

When we give the system the human's sound list instead of the detector's, the gate's F1 gain becomes significant (+0.067). So the ceiling is the sound detector — today's best models still miss sounds under speech and music.

Each pair is the same sound: “old” = the model in the videos above, “new” = the new model. One rater (me) saw each picture for 1.5 seconds, without knowing which model made it, and typed what makes the sound.

10 · Honest limits (so you hear them from me first)

Six things an examiner will ask about

  • Helpfulness for deaf viewers is not tested. All results are about sounds and pictures, scored against labels. No deaf viewer used the system. This goes in the abstract.
  • One annotator labelled all 139 clips. A second person on 30 clips is planned, to report agreement. This also goes in the abstract.
  • TEST was looked at more than once (5 planned reads, 10 exposures counting printouts), all dated and disclosed. The final table was scored once, under a rule written before.
  • The detector threshold was chosen on clips that overlap TEST (35 of 60). Both systems use it; a clean check on DEV picked the same value.
  • The caption baseline had no gate. A fair version (same gate, text instead of pictures) ties with pictures on the automatic judge.
  • Viewer cost was introduced after F1 came out a tie. Its weights date from before; we show results for every weight, not one chosen point.
11 · Three expert panels (27 Sept)

Three expert panels: what we will do this week

Three small panels (three experts each, three rounds) looked at detection, pictures and evaluation. All three signed a plan. One card per panel.

Detection

  • “Cascade E” (a looser detector) is not an upgrade: each extra sound it finds costs ≈ 2.4 false alarms. At β = 2 it would need 2.0 or fewer to pay.
  • One new model only: PE-A-Frame (Meta, open licence). It asks for each sound by name, but it hears with a different model than FlexSED, so its vote is new evidence.
  • Tested on a new held-out AudioSet set (500 clips, half of them busy scenes with speech or music). The rule is written before any number.
  • TEST is not touched. If it fails, the thesis reports a clear negative: the detector is the ceiling.

Pictures

  • The picture setup is frozen until the sealed blind sitting is scored.
  • The rules for “wrong object” are written and committed first. Nothing is added after a picture is seen.
  • 1–2 extra raters (no project knowledge) rate the same cards after me. Reported beside my score; mine stays the main one.
  • After scoring: fix the fading display (pictures now fade to 77 %; set to full strength), in its own commit.

Evaluation

  • Headline order: F1 first (a tie; smallest gain we could have seen: 0.13), then wrong pictures −0.52, then viewer cost for every β.
  • Precision is marked “TEST only”. The off-screen row is relabelled “detection + picture result, gate-free”.
  • The abstract says “one annotator” and “helpfulness for deaf viewers not tested”.
  • Human checks, in this order: second annotator > 3–5 glance raters > sound-off pilot (only if no ethics filing).

The panels agreed that no model change can be shown to help this week except one carefully tested detector check. So the week goes to honest reporting and human checks.
