\section{Threats to validity, limits and future work}\label{sec:limits}\label{sec:disclosures}

\subsection{What was pre-registered}
Every change to the system passed a rule written down before its run, on the development set; every rejected idea is
recorded with its result (Appendix~\ref{app:history}). The test set was never used to choose a change.

\subsection{Threats to validity}
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{T\arabic*.}, leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{The test set was seen.} It was read after each accepted change, about 19 times in the last week (and
        its 60 older clips 11 times before that). Twice a choice was influenced by a test read; both were undone. With
        many development variants and repeated test reads, the test numbers are somewhat optimistic and are a report on a
        seen set, not a blind confirmation.
  \item \textbf{Small sets.} 71 and 88 clips: one or two pictures can decide a development comparison, and the test set
        detects only cost differences of about 0.19 per clip or more.
  \item \textbf{One annotator.} All labels are the author's; no inter-annotator agreement exists. The author re-checked
        the visibility of 14 disputed development sounds blind (3 changed; 0 of 20 random controls changed), which may
        favour the system; test labels were never changed. Newer clips were labelled with detector suggestions visible,
        and 19 AudioSet clips with the AudioSet labels visible.
  \item \textbf{Strict ``wrong''.} It counts redundant and late pictures, and about 1 in 6 ``wrong'' pictures is a real
        off-screen sound missing from the labels.
  \item \textbf{Assumed cost weights.} A miss = 4, a wrong picture = 2 is an assumption chosen after F1 showed no
        difference; results for other weights are in Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}.
  \item \textbf{Final versus baseline not tested directly}, and the baseline was scored with a shorter repeat merge
        (Section~\ref{sec:headline}).
  \item \textbf{Scored versus live system.} The scored harness and the live pipeline differ in four small ways that do
        not change when a picture starts; one of them (the direction of a family rule) was not re-scored.
  \item \textbf{Pictures checked by the author only.} The picture claim rests on one blind glance test by the author;
        the automatic picture check was validated on known cases, not against a human rater.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limits}
No study with deaf or hard-of-hearing viewers was run, so the claim that the pictures help is untested. About one
needed sound in five cannot be found with today's detectors, and the gate confuses presence with production
(Section~\ref{sec:analysis}). The pipeline is heavy (about 5 minutes per clip of listener preparation on an H200 GPU,
about 20\,s per picture try), so it is an offline tool, not live captioning.

\subsection{Future work}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item A study with 5--10 DHH viewers (``helped / hurt / neither'' per clip) that \emph{measures} the cost of a miss
        against a wrong picture instead of assuming it (design in \fp{docs/history/plans/beta_specification.md}).
  \item A second annotator for 30 clips (the packet exists).
  \item The presence-versus-production question: a much larger vision model, a comparison of the sound's onset frame
        with a frame before it, or an external box around the named object.
  \item A detector that hears sounds under speech and music, tested on the unused fresh set of 422 AudioSet clips.
\end{enumerate}
