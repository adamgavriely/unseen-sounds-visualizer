\section{Development history}\label{app:history}

This appendix is the record of how the final system was reached: how the data grew, the working rules, the earlier
versions of the system, every accepted change, the main rejected ideas, the history of the pictures and of the
evaluation, and a round-by-round table. Internal codes from the development logs appear here in parentheses so
that each row can be found in \fp{docs/history/} of release v1.2.0 (the full development record,
Appendix~\ref{app:sources}); Appendix~\ref{app:glossary} maps them to plain names.

\subsection{How the data grew}\label{app:datahistory}
\paragraph{Clip-level benchmark.} 274 short clips (10--20\,s) were labelled by the author into four
scenarios: unseen ambient (48 clips), mixed (25), seen ambient (132) and no ambient (69). Four sourcing strategies
(keyword scraping, AudioSet strong segments, curated film scenes, UnAV-100 overlap moments) and an automatic filter
all landed at 15--20\,\% usable clips; with the resources of this project and no existing data that fits the task,
gathering enough correctly labelled footage was too hard, which is why the set stopped at 274 rather than the planned 300. A test set of
100 clips (25 per scenario) was used by the early systems and the model judge (Appendix~\ref{app:judge}).

\paragraph{Per-sound labels.} After the judge-based evaluation was abandoned, clips were labelled per
sound. The first export had 138 clips: 108 benchmark clips and 30 AudioSet-Strong clips with a consequential sound at
least half under speech or music (``slice B'', always reported separately). The 108 were split by provenance into 49
development clips (``DEV-49'', clips already touched by earlier runs) and 59 test clips (``TEST-59'', labelled after
the system was frozen). The earlier comparison table (Section~\ref{sec:sept-table}) is on the older test clips as they stood then.

\paragraph{New clips.} 50 more clips were labelled with a stand-alone tool in three
batches: 31 YouTube clips found by scene type and 19 AudioSet-Strong evaluation clips. They were split into 22
development (``DEV2'') and 28 test (``TEST2'') clips by a hash of the clip name or by balanced pairs, and then merged
into the development set (49 + 22 = 71 clips) and the test set (59 + 28 = 87 clips) used in the body.

\paragraph{Needed-sound count.} The development count moved in the last rounds: 55 at the first merged scoring, 58
after the visibility re-check (three sounds changed from seen to needed), 57 after a control round, and back to 58 after
a correction. Every system was re-scored on each version. The test count was 65 until five test labels were corrected
after the test outputs had been scored (Section~\ref{sec:limits}). The corrections come from a blind re-listen of the
test set: 92 questions at 76 moments in 51 test clips, built from the outputs of four systems (the final system, direct
audio-to-image generation and the two Qwen3-Omni one-model baselines) with the systems' names hidden. The questions ran in both
directions: every needed sound that at most one of the four had found, and every wrong picture of the final system or
of direct audio-to-image generation, or shown by two or more systems (pictures already judged in the blind rating of
round 46 were not asked again; the one laugh that rating had called a real off-screen sound was confirmed on the
second listen and is one of the two added). Only answers confirmed on a second listen were applied: laughter off screen was added
in two clips, a car horn that is not in the audio was removed, and two sounds were renamed (a buzzer labelled as an
electric toothbrush, and a motorcycle labelled as a chainsaw). Three answers that were not confirmed on the second
listen (an explosion first heard as on screen, a crow call, and one more car horn) left their labels unchanged. The
times of added sounds were measured from the audio, never from a system's output. The test set now has 66 needed
sounds and the benchmark 309 sounds. Every system and every test read of this appendix was re-scored from its saved
outputs on the corrected labels, except the earlier comparison of the on-screen check and the repeat-merge table, which are on the labels
of the time. Change list and both label files: \fp{benchmark/gold/annotations/} (README there).

\paragraph{AudioSet sets.} A calibration set of 280 AudioSet-Strong clips decided detector rounds 4--12 with a cost on
timed AudioSet labels. Round 12 showed that under a corrected version of that cost the detector of the time was worse
than showing nothing (1.650 vs 0.986 per clip), because AudioSet labels only a few sounds per clip; the set was retired
as a decision test. The screening set of 415 clips was then used only for single audio rules, and a fresh set of 422
clips (340 scored; the last two parts dropped for time before any score was read) was scored once after the system
was final, with nothing tuned on it: 12 hits of 65 labelled sounds and 31 pictures outside the labels for the final
system, 21 and 62 for direct audio-to-image (cost 0.806 vs 0.882, $p$ 0.14, paired clip bootstrap); as round 12 found,
this labelling does not separate any system from showing nothing (0.765). Script and results (field
\texttt{diff\_report\_rule}):
\fp{benchmark/gold/fresh_side_by_side.py}, \fp{benchmark/gold/fresh_side_by_side.json}.

\subsection{Working rules}\label{sec:rules}
From the robustness plan on, the following rules governed every change (\fp{docs/history/plans/plan_robustness.md},
\fp{docs/history/preregistrations/prereg_round13_detector_push.md}):
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Pre-register.} Before a run, write the idea, the exact rule, the data it is decided on and the pass
        bar, and commit it. Results are appended below the pre-registration, never edited into it.
  \item \textbf{Decide on development data only.} The development set decides; audio-only rules may first be
        screened, and stopped, on the held-out 415. The test set is scored after a change ships and is only reported.
  \item \textbf{General rules only.} A rule must make sense for any video, run on one video at a time, and not name a
        clip or a single sound class (a rule that only ever fires on bells was rejected for this reason, Round 64).
  \item \textbf{Compare with the shipped pipeline.} Every candidate is scored as a full-pipeline variant on the same
        clips as the current shipped system, not on a proxy; a component screen may stop an idea early but cannot
        ship it.
  \item \textbf{Hits first.} Losing a few hits is acceptable only if many wrong pictures go (pass rule (b),
        Section~\ref{sec:passrules}).
  \item \textbf{Consult and record.} Major choices were reviewed by panels of large-language-model reviewers (Claude, with assigned roles, two to five per panel, one to four rounds), whose reports are in
        \fp{docs/history/review/} and \fp{docs/history/panels/}; every decision and its reason is in a dated log.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The main lesson.} Two patterns recur in this table. First, the missing sounds can often be found -- a
listener asked about the whole clip names the hammer, the train, the bell -- but most ways of adding them bring two
or more wrong pictures per hit, because nothing downstream can tell a right off-screen name from a wrong one. Second,
every change to the gate moves along the same trade-off line: it silences more on-screen sounds only by silencing
about as many needed ones. Section~\ref{sec:analysis} measures both.

\subsection{Pictures}\label{sec:pic-history}
The picture model changed four times: Openverse retrieval (v1), SDXL and SDXL-Turbo, FLUX.1-schnell (chosen over
PixArt-$\Sigma$ on twelve real prompts), and Qwen-Image-2512 (frozen as the picture setup, then shipped). Earlier, a
retrieval-versus-SDXL ablation on the same 100 clips favoured retrieval by $+0.17$ (gated) and $+0.18$ (blind) judge
points, but 55\,\% of retrieved images carried a non-commercial or no-derivatives licence and 595 uses drew on only 60
distinct images, so generation was kept. The decisive picture test was a blind \emph{glance test} by the author:
54 sounds from 50 never-annotated clips, each picture shown for 1.5\,s at 384\,px with no name, and the author
typed what the sound was. FLUX was recognised 14/54 times; Qwen-Image with the same text 26/54
($+0.222\ [+0.074, +0.370]$); Qwen-Image with a richer subject text 32/54, but that text invented an object (a thud
drawn as a door) and was rejected. One of the 26 Qwen-Image pictures was later found to send a false message (a
telephone bell drawn as a desk bell), so the $+22$-point result is reported as ``not clean''. A planned sealed
confirmation sitting was cancelled and replaced by the check-and-redraw loop of Section~\ref{sec:pictures}.
A wording test run at the same time kept the hand-written subject table: 7 of 7 known cases right, against 3 of 7 for two
generic VLM wording methods (\fp{docs/history/analyses/picture_sense_test_2026-09-28.md}).

\subsection{Earlier evaluation by model judge (superseded)}\label{app:judge}
The proposal planned an automatic evaluation: a VLM describes the augmented video, an LLM writes a reference of what
the viewer should learn, and an independent LLM scores the description against the reference on a 0--4 scale. It
was built and run, and it failed in instructive ways:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A reference written by the system's own models is circular.} The first reference was written by the
        system's VLM from the system's detections; it rewarded whichever system repeated the detector (blind 2.94 $>$
        caption 2.68 $>$ gated 2.41). A second judge of another family agreed with the first
        (quadratic $\kappa = 0.753$), which shows only that two judges can share a bias.
  \item \textbf{A reference written by independent models cannot decide visibility.} A reference built by an audio
        LLM, CLAP, a different VLM and a different LLM agreed with the human ``nothing is missing'' label on 55 of 100
        clips -- chance. An audio-visual model asked with and without the soundtrack reached 49.5\,\% balanced
        accuracy (bar 67.7\,\%); a ``gap-closing'' score had AUROC 0.529 (bar 0.75).
  \item \textbf{The rubric was asymmetric.} A withheld picture scored 0 where one was due, a redundant one about 3
        where none was due, so the rubric punished staying silent -- the very behaviour the gate exists for. A capped
        rubric and a human-grounded reference fixed the direction but not the noise.
  \item \textbf{Judges cannot tell a picture from a text tag.} The best judge (Gemma-4-31B seeing the pictures
        directly, passing all three trust checks against the author's labels) scored pictures and text tags alike
        ($-0.04\ [-0.22, +0.14]$), because it reads a tag as the label. Six automatic picture checkers also failed
        calibration against the author (for example $\kappa = 0.21$ to $0.45$ against bars of 0.5).
\end{enumerate}
A five-reviewer scoring panel then moved the primary score to per-sound matching against human labels
(Section~\ref{sec:task}) and kept the judge as a secondary instrument. The answer to RQ4 is built on this history.


\subsection{How the cost changed during development}\label{app:progression}
Figure~\ref{fig:progression} (Section~\ref{sec:scale}) shows the cost after each accepted change, from the first full
pipeline to the final system. The development cost fell by 1.634 per clip and the test cost by 0.575;
the gap is expected, because changes were chosen on the development set.

The last step deserves a note. The witness rule was first scored with its scene question read as text, which gave 2.028
(development) and 2.368 (test). That text answer was sometimes cut off after four words, and one cut-off answer happened
to remove a wrong picture -- an artefact of truncation, not a property of the rule. The final system reads the same question from
the model's yes/no scores, which cannot be cut off; this brings the wrong picture back and gives the reported numbers 2.056
and 2.391. The final system was accepted because it beats the version before the witness rule (2.141 on development),
which was the pre-set bar; the text-read result is therefore not plotted as a separate step.
