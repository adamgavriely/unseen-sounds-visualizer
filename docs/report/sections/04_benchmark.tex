\section{Data}\label{sec:benchmark}

All results use two sets of short video clips (10--20\,s), labelled per sound as in Section~\ref{sec:needed}:
\begin{description}
  \item[Development set] 71 clips, 58 needed sounds. Every change was accepted on this set.
  \item[Test set] 88 clips, 65 needed sounds. Scored only after a change was accepted, and only reported.
\end{description}
The clips come from Wikimedia Commons, the Internet Archive, UnAV-100~\cite{geng2023unav100} (its videos are used as
scene material; its labels are not used), VGGSound~\cite{chen2020vggsound},
YouTube (city walks, markets, home, transport, incidents, film scenes) and the AudioSet-Strong evaluation split (AudioSet clips that come with timed, per-sound labels). New
clips were assigned to development or test by a hash of the clip name or in pairs matched by scene, one to each set, never by
looking at results. The first 109 clips were split by provenance instead: clips that earlier runs had already used became development clips, and clips no run had touched became test clips. A later scene search therefore sits in the test set only: 21 clips, many of them film scenes, war footage and body-, helmet- or dash-camera footage, kinds of scene the development set hardly contains. Appendix~\ref{app:datahistory} describes how the sets grew. Most clips are copyrighted and used for research only, so they are not redistributed
(see \hyperref[sec:availability]{Code and data availability}).

About half of the clips have no needed sound; on those, the best possible behaviour is to show nothing. Three needed
development sounds (a clang and two golf-club strikes) belong to labels the system never draws (the label filter removes
generic impact sounds, Appendix~\ref{app:config}), so they are misses for every system.

\paragraph{Label quality.} All labels are the author's; a second annotator was planned but did not return labels, so
inter-annotator agreement is unknown. On the earlier clip-level labels, a blind relabelling by the author agreed
78\,\% of the time (Cohen's $\kappa = 0.60$; $\kappa$ discounts chance agreement, 1 is perfect, 0 is chance). That is
the author's agreement with their own earlier clip-level labels, not per-sound agreement with a second annotator. These and other caveats are listed
in Section~\ref{sec:limits}.

\paragraph{Screening set.} 415 clips of the AudioSet-Strong evaluation split, which already have timed labels, were
used to screen rules about audio alone (for example: how often is a detection that no listener names a real sound?).
AudioSet labels only a few sounds per clip, so this set was never used to accept a change.
