\section{Benchmark and labels}\label{sec:benchmark}

\subsection{Data sets}
All final numbers are on two sets of short video clips (10--20\,s) labelled per sound with the fields of
Section~\ref{sec:needed}:
\begin{description}
  \item[The development set] 71 clips with 58 needed sounds. Every design decision was made on this set.
  \item[The test set] 88 clips with 65 needed sounds, read after a change was accepted and only reported.
\end{description}
Both sets mix three sources: clips of an earlier clip-level benchmark (Wikimedia Commons, the Internet Archive,
UnAV-100~\cite{geng2023unav100}, VGGSound~\cite{chen2020vggsound} controls and YouTube, including film scenes); new
YouTube clips found by scene type (transport, city walk, market, nature, home, incident, work event, water, film/TV;
one 15--20-s cut per upload); and 10-s clips from the AudioSet-Strong evaluation split. The sets grew in two steps,
described in Appendix~\ref{app:datahistory}; new clips were assigned to development or test by a hash of the clip
name or by balanced pairs, never by looking at results. Most clips are for research use only and are not
redistributed.

The last column counts clips by scenario: \emph{unseen} (an ambient sound whose source is off screen), \emph{mixed}
(some sources visible, some not), \emph{seen} (every source on screen) and \emph{none} (speech or music only). About half
of the clips (33 of 71 and 45 of 88) have no needed sound at all; on them, the best a system can do is show nothing.

\paragraph{Three unreachable sounds.} The labels keep every non-speech sound, while the final system's label filter
(Section~\ref{sec:labelfilter}) never draws three of the development labels (\emph{Clang} in one clip and two
\emph{Whack, thwack} rows in another). They are misses for every system by construction; without them the development
set would have 55 needed sounds.

\paragraph{Other data.} Two further sets were used for narrower questions:
\begin{description}
  \item[The screening set] 415 clips from the AudioSet-Strong evaluation split (252 ``complex'' clips where speech or
        music covers half the clip and a sound lies under it, 163 random). Its 0.1-s labels need no new annotation, so
        it was used to screen rules about the audio alone (for example ``how often is a detection that no listener
        names a real sound?''). It never decided a change by itself, because AudioSet labels only a few sounds per clip:
        a cost computed on it counted most correct detections as false.
  \item[DCASE 2025 Task 3] 258 events with on/off-screen labels, used once as an outside check of the on-screen
        decision of an earlier version (66.7\,\% agreement; the DCASE label is geometric, so it bounds the gate only
        loosely).
\end{description}

\subsection{Label quality}\label{sec:recheck}
All labels are the author's. A packet of 30 clips for a second annotator (stratified, half development, half test)
was built but no labels came back, so inter-rater agreement is unknown. The only reliability number is intra-rater,
on the earlier clip-level labels: 60 clips relabelled blind, 78\,\% agreement, Cohen's $\kappa = 0.60$. Three
further facts bear on label quality (all listed again in Section~\ref{sec:disclosures}):
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Anchoring.} Earlier clips were pre-filled from the system under test, the labelling tool showed
        detector suggestions on 37 of the 50 newest clips, and the 19 AudioSet-Strong clips were labelled with the
        AudioSet labels available behind a button (shown on 16 of 19).
  \item \textbf{Visibility re-check.} The author re-judged, blind to the old answer, 14 borderline development sounds
        where the system and the labels disagreed; 3 changed from seen to needed (a church bell, a fire alarm, a
        thunder clap). A control round on 20 random development sounds changed none. The test labels were never
        changed.
  \item \textbf{Missing sounds.} In a blind check of pictures counted as wrong, about 1 in 6 turned out to be a real,
        wanted, off-screen sound the labels do not list. No label was edited; absolute costs are therefore slightly
        pessimistic for every system alike.
\end{itemize}
The labelling guide for the newest clips adds two rules: a sound that stops for more than about 2\,s and comes back
gets a new row, and a long sound whose visibility changes is split into two rows.
