\section{Task definition and metric}\label{sec:task}

\subsection{What counts as a needed sound}\label{sec:needed}
The unit of evaluation is one sound in one clip. For every sound the annotator records a label (mapped to the
AudioSet ontology), its onset and offset (about 0.5-s precision), and four judgements
(\fp{benchmark/gold/tool_template.html}, \fp{docs/history/plans/metric_per_sound.md}):

\begin{description}[style=nextline]
  \item[visible] the thing making the sound is \emph{seen making it now}. A barking dog on screen is visible; a
        baby held quietly is not visibly crying; an alarm box on a wall is not visibly ringing. If the source is
        visible for only part of the sound, tick visible only if it is visible for most of it.
  \item[obvious] ``with the sound off, would a viewer still know this sound is happening now?'' (for example
        waves when the sea fills the frame).
  \item[importance] a property of the sound, rated as if the screen were black: 1 = steady noise of the place,
        no clear start or end; 2 = an event that can be said in one sentence; 3 = danger or a key story moment
        (siren, alarm, explosion, gunshot, glass, baby crying, doorbell, phone, horn, scream). When unsure, the
        lower value.
  \item[masked] the sound is covered by speech or music (recorded, not used in scoring).
\end{description}

A sound is \textbf{needed} if it is neither visible nor obvious (\fp{benchmark/gold/score_per_sound.py},
\cfgv{load_gold}), and it is \textbf{scored} if its importance is 2 or 3 (\cfgv{MIN_IMPORTANCE = 2}). A needed
sound of importance 1 is ``don't care'': a picture on it is neither a hit nor wrong. Speech and music are never
sounds. Every heard sound is labelled, not only the needed ones, because a picture with no labelled sound under it
counts as wrong.

\paragraph{Example.} In a clip of a street at night, a police siren is heard while the camera shows a crowd
looking up. The siren is not visible and not obvious from the picture, and it is importance 3: it is
\emph{needed}. If the police car then drives into view with its lights on, a second siren row is labelled visible.

\subsection{Matching pictures to sounds}\label{sec:matching}
Pictures are scored as they are \emph{displayed}: the scorer replays the compositor's display rules (repeat merge,
minimum time on screen, grouping, at most three slots) before matching (\cfgv{load_pictures}). The rules, from
\cfgv{score_clip}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Onset window.} A picture can match a sound only if the picture \emph{starts} within
        $[t_\text{onset}-0.5\,\text{s},\ t_\text{onset}+1.0\,\text{s}]$. The end of the picture is not scored.
        (A scoring panel first proposed $+2$\,s; the author set $+1.0$\,s on 19 Sept, before any per-sound score.)
  \item \textbf{Same family.} The picture's label and the sound's label must be the same, or one must be an
        ontology ancestor of the other, and both must be below the top level of the ontology (depth $\geq 1$):
        \emph{Vehicle} matches \emph{Car}, but the top-level \emph{Sounds of things} matches nothing.
  \item \textbf{One to one.} Each sound takes at most one picture (the nearest in time). Extra pictures on a sound
        already taken are \emph{duplicates}: not credited, not counted as wrong.
\end{enumerate}

Each scored needed sound is a \textbf{hit} or a \textbf{miss}. Each picture that is not a hit or a duplicate is
\textbf{wrong}, of one of three kinds:
\begin{description}
  \item[on screen (``visible'')] it matches a sound that is not needed (visible or obvious). The viewer could see
        it; the picture is redundant.
  \item[other sound (``cross'')] no same-family sound starts in its window, but some labelled sound is playing or
        starting there. This includes a second picture of a long sound that started earlier and is still playing --
        the \emph{strict} rule.
  \item[nothing (``phantom'')] no labelled sound at all is there.
\end{description}
``Wrong'' is therefore strict: it counts unneeded pictures, not only false ones. Section~\ref{sec:disclosures}
gives a human check of how often a ``wrong'' picture is in fact a real sound the labels missed.

\subsection{Viewer cost}\label{sec:metric}
The decision metric is the viewer cost per clip,
\begin{equation}
  C \;=\; \frac{4\cdot\#\text{missed} \;+\; \beta\cdot\#\text{wrong}}{\#\text{clips}},\qquad \beta = 2,
  \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
lower is better (\cfgv{COST_MISS, COST_FA = 4.0, 2.0} in \fp{score_per_sound.py}). A missed needed sound costs
twice a wrong picture: one hit pays for at most two wrong pictures, so a picture is worth showing when the chance
that it is right exceeds $\beta/(4+\beta) = 1/3$. The weights come from the rubric of the September judge
(a withheld picture scored 0 where one was due, a redundant one about 3 of 4) and were declared for a gate sweep
before any per-sound result; they were promoted to a reported outcome only after per-sound F1 came out null
(22 Sept), so the choice of $\beta = 2$ is \emph{post hoc} and is reported with sensitivity.

\paragraph{On-screen weight $w$.} A picture of a sound whose source is on screen is less harmful than a picture of
nothing. From 1 Oct the cost is also reported as
\begin{equation}
  C_w \;=\; \frac{4\cdot\#\text{missed} + w\cdot\#\text{visible} + 2\cdot\#\text{cross} + 2\cdot\#\text{phantom}}{\#\text{clips}},
  \qquad w\in\{0,1,2\},
  \label{eq:costw}
\end{equation}
with $w=2$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:cost}) as the primary. Wins that hold only at $w=1$ were to go to a separate ``more hits''
profile, never to the main system.

\paragraph{Why not F1.} Per-sound F1 on the same labels was the pre-registered headline from 22 Sept. It weighs a
miss and a wrong picture equally, and the gate's value lies almost entirely in the wrong pictures it removes, so the
F1 difference between the gated system and the blind baseline was null in every version (for example
$+0.059\ [-0.030, +0.144]$, $p = 0.18$, on the September test table) while the number of wrong pictures fell
significantly (Section~\ref{sec:sept-table}). F1, precision and recall are still computed by the scorer.

\subsection{Statistics}\label{sec:stats}
All comparisons are paired by clip. A \textbf{paired clip bootstrap} (2000 draws, seed 0) resamples clips and scores
every system on the same resampled clips. Two read-outs are used, and the report always says which:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Development comparisons}: mean per-clip cost
        difference $d$, a 95\,\% percentile interval, and a \textbf{one-sided} $p$ = share of draws with
        $d \geq 0$. All $p$-values of the final development phase are of this kind; a two-sided value is about
        twice as large.
  \item \emph{September test table}: \textbf{two-sided}
        $p = \min(1, 2\min(P(d\geq0), P(d\leq0)))$ with Holm step-down correction at $\alpha = 0.05$ over two
        pre-declared families (7 and 3 comparisons).
\end{itemize}
With 88 test clips the smallest cost difference this design can detect is about 0.19 per clip; a difference of
0.09 would need about 385 clips. Most single changes made during development (Appendix~\ref{app:history}) are smaller
than that, which is why decisions were made on pre-declared rules on the development set and not on test
significance.

\subsection{Pass rules on the development set}\label{sec:passrules}
A change to the system was accepted only if it passed a rule written before its run, on the development set:
\begin{description}
  \item[main rule] hits $\geq$ before, wrong $\leq$ before $+\,2\times$(hits gained), cost $<$ before, and no needed
        hit lost;
  \item[fewer-pictures rule] cost $<$ before, at least three wrong pictures removed per hit lost, and at most three
        hits lost;
  \item[screening] a rule about the audio alone could first be screened on the screening set
        (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}) and stopped there, but never accepted on it.
\end{description}
The test set was read only after a change was accepted, and only reported. Section~\ref{sec:disclosures} counts how often that happened.
