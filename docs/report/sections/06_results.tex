In words: on the test set the final system finds 37\,\% of the needed sounds and half of its pictures are right; on the
development set the numbers are 50\,\% and two thirds. The September baseline was worse than showing nothing on the
development set and about equal to it on the test set. The final system is significantly better than showing nothing on
both.

\fbox{\begin{minipage}{0.93\linewidth}\small
\textbf{What is and is not statistically supported.}
\textbf{(1) Tested and significant:} the final system costs less than showing nothing, on the development set
($p < 0.001$) and on the test set ($p = 0.047$, two-sided). The on-screen check removes wrong pictures

\textbf{(2) Supported indirectly:} the final system costs less than the September baseline. The direct paired test was

system is cheaper than that version on both sets.

as a blind estimate, because the test set was read after each accepted change (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\subsection{Does the on-screen check help?}\label{sec:sept-table}
The gate is the central idea of the system: draw a sound only if its source is not visible. Its value was measured
directly on the September test set (60 clips, the baseline's detector, the same gate model and rule) by comparing the
system with and without the gate:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering\small
\caption{With minus without the on-screen check (September test set, 60 clips). Two-sided $p$ with Holm correction.}
\label{tab:sept}
\begin{tabular}{lrrl}
\toprule
measure & difference & 95\,\% interval & Holm $p$ \\
\midrule
wrong pictures per clip & $-0.517$ & $[-0.800,\ -0.283]$ & $<0.001$ \\
viewer cost per clip & $-0.833$ & & 0.012 \\
precision (share of pictures that are right) & $+0.137$ & & 0.010 \\
clips handled perfectly & $+0.219$ & & 0.006 \\
F1 (counts a miss and a wrong picture the same) & $+0.059$ & $[-0.030,\ +0.144]$ & 0.18 (not significant) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The gate removes about half a wrong picture per clip and loses little: F1 does not move because what the gate gains
lies almost entirely in the pictures it does \emph{not} show. On clips whose sound is off screen, the gated system was
also far cheaper than showing nothing ($-3.08$ per clip, $p < 0.001$). The comparison was not repeated with the final
detector.

\subsection{Sensitivity}\label{sec:sensitivity}
If an on-screen picture costs 1 or 0 instead of 2 (Eq.~\ref{eq:costw}), the final system costs 1.97 or 1.89
(development) and 2.36 or 2.32 (test); every accepted change lowered the cost at every such weight, so no decision depends
on it. On the September test set the gated system was the cheapest option for any wrong-picture weight between 0.39 and
2.56 (2 is the one used here).

\subsection{Where the errors come from}\label{sec:errors}
Of the 41 test misses, 32 were heard as candidates and then removed by a later rule (most often because the listeners
did not confirm a weak detection), 6 were heard by no detector, and 3 were heard but timed outside the window. Of the 24
test wrong pictures, 15 show another sound, 5 show nothing and 4 show an on-screen sound. Section~\ref{sec:analysis}
explains why.
