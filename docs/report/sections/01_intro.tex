\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Motivation}
Deaf and hard-of-hearing (DHH) viewers receive speech through subtitles and, where available, sign language.
Neither channel is built for the rest of the soundtrack. Subtitles for the deaf and hard of hearing (SDH) may
name a sound effect in brackets, but the DCMP Captioning Key~\cite{dcmp} tells captioners that the source of a
sound ``may be omitted if it can be clearly seen onscreen'', and that background effects are written only when
they matter to the plot. A study of more than 715{,}000 YouTube videos found hand-written non-speech captions in
only 5--7\,\% of them, and environmental sounds in 2--3\,\%~\cite{may2024unspoken}. Field studies of
sound-awareness tools~\cite{jain2019home,homesound,soundwatch} show that DHH users want to know about exactly the
sounds that carry information -- knocks, alarms, sirens, a baby crying, a dog barking -- and interviews on
non-speech captions report that viewers want the type and the timing of story-relevant sounds~\cite{may2025choices}.

The information a hearing viewer gets from an ambient sound is therefore lost in proportion to how far its
source is from the frame. A siren over a visible ambulance tells the viewer nothing new; a siren over a crowd
looking up \emph{is} the scene. This project targets the second case. It differs from audio-to-image generation
\cite{sungbin2023sound2scene,biner2024sonicdiffusion}, which turns a whole soundtrack into a picture regardless of
the video, and from sound-source localisation~\cite{senocak2023alignment,um2025objaware}, which asks where in the
frame a sound comes from but not whether the viewer needs to be told about it.

\subsection{Problem statement}\label{sec:problem}
Given a video, produce the same video with a side panel that shows, while an ambient sound is heard whose source
the viewer cannot see, a picture of that sound -- and shows nothing otherwise. Speech and music are never drawn
(they have their own channels). Human non-word sounds such as laughter, coughing or a scream \emph{are} drawn.
No model is trained or fine-tuned: the system is an inference pipeline over open models, and human work is
limited to the labels used for evaluation. The system must run on one video at a time (``online''): every rule
has to work without seeing other clips, and every expensive step needs a fixed trigger.

Concretely, for every sound in a clip the system must answer four questions:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Is there a sound?} Find each non-speech, non-music sound and when it starts.
  \item \textbf{What is it?} Name it at a level a picture can show (for example \emph{dog bark}, not \emph{animal}).
  \item \textbf{Can the viewer already see it?} If the thing making the sound is visibly making it, stay silent.
  \item \textbf{What should the picture show, and for how long?}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Research questions}\label{sec:rqs}
The research questions are those of the project proposal (6 August 2026); Section~\ref{sec:conclusion} gives
the answer to each.
\begin{enumerate}[label=RQ\arabic*.]
  \item Which types of audio information contribute most to scene understanding when presented visually?
  \item What level of semantic granularity should be used to represent ambient sounds?
  \item Can automatically generated visual augmentations improve accessibility beyond subtitles?
  \item How should semantic audio augmentation systems be evaluated?
  \item How does the proposed pipeline compare with representative audio-to-visual approaches?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{A task definition} with a precise rule for when a picture is due: a sound is \emph{needed} when
        it is neither visible (``seen making the sound now'') nor obvious (``with the sound off, the viewer
        would still know it is happening''), and it is an event, not steady background
        (Section~\ref{sec:task}).
  \item \textbf{A per-sound benchmark}: a development set of 71 clips and a test set of 88 clips with timed labels,
        visibility and importance for every sound (Section~\ref{sec:benchmark}).
  \item \textbf{A metric that matches the decision}: hits, wrong pictures split by cause, and a viewer cost
        that prices a missed sound at twice a wrong picture, with a sensitivity scale for on-screen pictures
        (Section~\ref{sec:metric}).
  \item \textbf{A working system} of open models with no training, runnable on one video (\fp{main.py}; a
        ComfyUI wrapper is in \fp{comfyui_nodes/}) (Section~\ref{sec:system}).
  \item \textbf{An error analysis} that locates the limit of today's open models for this task: sounds no model
        hears, and the confusion between an object being present and the object making the sound
        (Section~\ref{sec:analysis}).
  \item \textbf{A complete, pre-registered development record}: every idea tried, its pass bar written before the
        run, its result and the reason it was kept or dropped, including negative results about automatic
        evaluation with model judges (Appendix~\ref{app:history}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{How to read this report}
This is a technical report in the sense of the course guidelines: it aims at a depth that lets a reader
reproduce the system and its numbers. The main body describes the \textbf{final system} only.
Section~\ref{sec:related} places the work. Sections~\ref{sec:task} and~\ref{sec:benchmark} define the task, the
metric and the data. Section~\ref{sec:system} describes the final system stage by stage with every threshold,
Section~\ref{sec:results} gives the results, Section~\ref{sec:analysis} the error analysis,
Section~\ref{sec:disclosures} the threats to validity, Section~\ref{sec:limits} limitations and future work, and
Section~\ref{sec:repro} exact reproduction steps.

The final system was reached through a pre-registered development process of about seventy rounds. That process,
the earlier versions of the system and every rejected idea are described in Appendix~\ref{app:history}; the body
refers to it only where a rejected alternative explains a design choice. Appendix~\ref{app:sources} lists the
result file behind every number in the body, and Appendix~\ref{app:glossary} maps the short internal names used in
the development logs to the plain names used here.

\paragraph{Plain-language summary.} The system listens to a video and looks at it. When it hears something
(for example a dog barking) it asks: can the viewer already see the dog barking? If yes, it does nothing. If
no, it draws a picture of a barking dog and shows it next to the video while the bark is heard. On the test
videos it finds about 37\,\% of the sounds a deaf viewer would need, and about half of the pictures it shows are
right. Most of what it misses, it misses because no current open model hears the sound under speech and music,
or because the vision model believes a visible object is the source when it is not.

\paragraph{Names used in this report.} \emph{The final system} is the code at git tag
\texttt{detector-frozen-2026-10-02}, configured by \cfgv{config.use_shipped()}. \emph{The September baseline}
