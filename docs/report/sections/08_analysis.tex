\section{Error analysis}\label{sec:analysis}

This section explains \emph{why} the remaining errors happen, using the per-item ledgers
(\fp{benchmark/gold/ledger_SHIP8_MD3_WW5.json}), the VLM gate audit of 1 Oct
(\fp{docs/review/vlm_panel_2026-10-01/}), the two expert panels of 1 Oct (\fp{docs/review/panel_2026-10-01/},
\fp{docs/review/panel2_2026-10-01/}) and the Decision Inspector.

\subsection{Failure modes}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Presence taken for production.} The gate's commonest error: an object being in the frame is taken as
        that object making the sound now. Examples from the development set: a church tower is visible, so the ringing
        bell is silenced although nothing in the frames shows it ringing; a golfer stands over the ball, so the whack
        that happens later is called visible; perched macaws are taken as the source of a bird call made off screen.
        In the audit this class explains 23 of 36 gate errors across development and test. Two of the three votes
        effectively measure presence (``name the thing'' echoes the label on 30 of 54 development ``seen'' stretches,
        and the follow-up ``could that thing make it?'' is answered from world knowledge), so the majority vote measures
        presence too.
  \item \textbf{The ``nothing'' escape.} The naming question allows ``nothing'', and the VLM answers it on 42\,\% of the
        stretches the gold calls seen (32 of 76): the maker is small, hidden, or its action is fine (a hand, a coin), the
        camera is the walker (footsteps), or only the rain is visible during thunder. These sounds keep their picture;
        this explains 32 of the 38 development gate misses (wrongly kept on-screen sounds).
  \item \textbf{Masking.} Needed sounds under speech or music are scored low by BEATs, whose top labels there are
        \emph{Speech} or \emph{Music}. FlexSED hears more of them but mostly below its bar; the listener rescue recovers
        some, at a price in wrong pictures (Section~\ref{sec:history}).
  \item \textbf{Cross pictures.} On the test set 15 of 24 wrong pictures belong to another sound. Examples from the
        development ledger: a gunshot picture over footsteps, a crowd picture over laughter, a scream picture over a
        bird call. These are mostly confident
        detector errors with a plausible scene, which neither the witness rule nor the gate can refuse.
  \item \textbf{Timing and repeats.} A sound that pauses for more than 2\,s and returns is a new labelled event; the
        system either merges it (a miss) or draws a late second picture of a long sound (a cross). Neither the listener
        nor the detectors can tell a 2-s pause from a dropout (Rounds 51, 55).
  \item \textbf{Read-out artefacts.} Asked to answer ``(a)'' or ``(b)'', the 27B model favours a position (``(a)'' on
        88\,\% of answers in Round 54); asked yes/no, it has a ``no'' habit; asked for free text, its answer can be cut off.
        The fix used in \Dp{} is to read the yes/no logits of the first token, minus those of a negated twin question.
        This removes the artefacts but not the underlying weakness: as a gate signal the logit margin reaches AUROC
        0.647, under the pre-set 0.65 bar.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The central limit: ``present'' is not ``making the sound''}\label{sec:presence}
Every attempt to improve the gate in the project landed on the same trade-off line. In September an OWLv2 vote, a SAM 3
vote and a looser silence rule each removed exactly two wrong pictures for every real needed sound they lost, which is
break-even at $\beta = 2$. In the October rounds a box-and-crop check, an audio-visual synchrony model, an
audio-visual localiser, a human-style ``name, find, zoom, ask'' chain and a larger or different VLM each silenced
more on-screen sounds only by silencing about as many needed ones. A model swap from Qwen2.5-VL-7B to Qwen3.8-27B moved
balanced accuracy from 0.61 to 0.62, and Gemma-4-31B moved along the same line. The VLM audit concluded ``not the
model'': what is asked of the frames -- \emph{is this object making this sound right now?} -- is a question about an
action at an instant, and still frames from open VLMs verify presence well and action poorly.

Two observations make the limit concrete. First, the gold's own notion of \emph{obvious} encodes inference the
gate does not do: a bell in a tower is \emph{not} obvious (one cannot see it ring), thunder under visible rain
\emph{is}. A rule that treats ``concealed actions'' as not visible passed the development test (Round 64: 30 / 14 /
1.972) but in practice fired only on bells, and was not shipped as a general rule. Second, a larger vision model
(Qwen3-VL-235B) was the only untested lever named by every panel; it does not fit the available disk and is left as
future work. The audit's own reading is that a larger model should be tested on the presence-versus-production
question, not assumed to fix it.

\subsection{The detector floor}
The ceiling analysis (Section~\ref{sec:ceiling}) puts a hard limit on any decision layer: about one needed sound in
eleven is heard by no model at all, and about one in eight more is too faint or too mistimed to recover. Every
attempt to hear more of them (Section~\ref{sec:rejected}) found real sounds -- 80\,\% of what the best listener chain
proposed on the held-out set was real -- but brought two to four wrong pictures per new hit on the video benchmark.
The reason is the combination of the two limits: a newly heard sound still has to pass the gate, and the gate cannot
tell a right off-screen sound from a wrong one.

\subsection{Is the gold too strict?}
Two human checks bound the effect of the strict ``wrong'' rule. In Round 46 the author rated, blind, samples of
``wrong'' pictures: about one in six (2/11 and 3/20) is a real, wanted, off-screen sound the gold does not list.
In Round 46b, 12 current wrong pictures were rated: 4 not heard, 5 real but with the source on screen, 2 real and
off-screen but timed away from the gold row, 1 unsure; no clean case for a gold edit was found. The ceiling analysis
adds that the 14 borderline gate-side items sit one judgement away from the other class: flipping all of them would
bring SHIP7's cost from 2.451 to about 1.9 with no change to the system. The absolute costs are therefore somewhat
pessimistic for every system; the comparisons between systems are not affected, because the same gold scores all of
them.

\subsection{A worked example}
Clip \fp{bell_miami} (development) has one needed sound: a \emph{Bell} from 0.2 to 14.5\,s, importance 3, labelled
not visible. Its trail in the Decision Inspector (\fp{docs/inspector2/data.js}) reads as follows. Stage 4 keeps
several \emph{Bell} and \emph{Church bell} candidates. The gate cuts the 14-s burst into three stretches of about
4.8\,s. In each, the name vote answers ``church bell'' (it shares the word \emph{bell} with the label: yes), the a/b vote
says not visibly happening (no), and the description vote says yes. Two of three in every stretch: the sound is
silenced, and the needed bell is a miss lost at the gate. The frames show the church; they do not show the bell
swinging. This single sound is the whole development gain of the bell rule of Round 64 (``a concealed action is not
visible''), and a clean example of presence taken for production: the one vote that asks about the action got it
right, and was outvoted by two votes that measure presence.
