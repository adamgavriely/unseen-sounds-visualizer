\subsection{Problem statement}
Given a video, produce the same video with a side panel that shows, for the duration of each
ambient sound whose source is not visible on screen, a picture of that sound's event -- and shows
nothing otherwise. Speech and music are excluded. No model is trained; the system is an inference
pipeline over open-source foundation models, and human involvement is limited to the benchmark
labels used for evaluation.

\subsection{Research questions}
% Verbatim from the proposal (Aug 6, 2026); each is answered in sec:discussion.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Which types of audio information contribute most to scene understanding when presented visually?
  \item What level of semantic granularity should be used to represent ambient sounds?
  \item Can automatically generated visual augmentations improve accessibility beyond subtitles?
  \item How should semantic audio augmentation systems be evaluated?
  \item How does the proposed pipeline compare with representative audio-to-visual approaches?
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Formulation of semantic audio augmentation as an accessibility task, with a precise
        definition of when a picture is due (sound heard, source not visible, not speech or music).
  \item A seven-stage inference pipeline of off-the-shelf models (\S\ref{sec:method}).
  \item A benchmark of 274 human-labelled clips in four scenarios (\S\ref{sec:benchmark}).
  \item An automatic evaluation protocol, and the finding that its reference must be independent
        of the system under test (\S\ref{sec:eval}).
  \item An experimental comparison with two baselines, with paired statistics and an oracle bound
        (\S\ref{sec:results}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Stage 4: detection and onset timing}
% BEATs replaced PANNs (notes sec:beats: 11/14 detector complaints resolved). 2 s window,
% hop 0.25 s, reflect lead-in pad, stamp offset 0.5 s. Threshold 0.35, set once (plan_robustness).
% Onset: hysteresis (0.5 of threshold) + prefix-silencing occlusion (notes sec:onset).
% CAM on BEATs tokens does not work (deltas exactly 0 / -1.5): recorded as a negative result.
\textbf{Detector.} Stage 4 tags audio with BEATs (iter3+, fine-tuned on AudioSet-2M; 0.486 mAP
against PANNs CNN14's 0.431 on the same 527 labels). BEATs is a clip-level tagger, so a 2\,s window
is scored every 0.25\,s and shaped like frame-wise output; a reflected lead-in pad lets a sound in
the first two seconds be heard, and a window's score is stamped 0.5\,s before its end so that an
onset is never early. The swap from PANNs was made after a review of eight demo clips traced eleven
of fourteen complaints to the detector's labels (a siren as ``truck horn'', a crying baby as
``sheep''); on the same clips every one resolved. Speech and music classes are never shown. The
display bar is 0.35, set once on the development split and not moved since (\S\ref{sec:process}).

\textbf{Onset.} A window cannot say where inside it a sound begins, and under the plain stamp every
onset on three hand-checked clips was late by $+0.25$ to $+2.0$\,s. Two standard pieces on the same
model fix most of it. \emph{Hysteresis}: a sound must reach the bar, then extends back through any
contiguous stretch above half of it (an approaching helicopter scores 0.15--0.42 for 1.5\,s before
crossing 0.35). \emph{Prefix-silencing occlusion} inside the first window that fired: silence its
opening $t$ seconds for 26 values of $t$, score them in one batched forward pass, and read the onset
as the first cut that removes a tenth of the class's evidence in logit space. It is class-conditional,
so a voice in the same window does not pull it. Three alternatives were measured and rejected: finer
windows and frame-level detectors (PANNs 10\,ms, BEATs-strong 40\,ms) miss about half the sounds; a
class-activation map over BEATs' 160\,ms tokens moves every onset by exactly $0$ or $-1.5$\,s,
because the model was fine-tuned only through the token mean and no token was ever asked to be local.
Results in \S\ref{sec:results}.

\subsection{Stage 5: is the source on screen?}
% Majority of three signals (open naming, world-knowledge check, open description), every
% two-way question asked in both orderings because the 7B model has a position bias; judged
% per 5 s stretch; one continuous picture per continuous sound (notes sec:vlmvisibility,
% sec:actionvisible). Speech as gate context, never as picture material (sec:speechgate).
The proposal's premise is that a picture is due only when the source of a sound is \emph{not} on
screen, so this question carries the system. It was first answered by a hand-written dictionary of
about thirty labels mapped to CLIP prompts; a sound outside the dictionary could never be marked
visible, so laughter, footsteps and a telephone were structurally ungated. It is now asked at run
time about the sound that was actually detected, from frames sampled across that sound's own span.

\textbf{Name it, do not confirm it.} The VLM (Qwen2.5-VL-7B) is asked to name the thing in the
frames making that sound, or ``nothing''. A yes/no question to a VLM collects agreement rather than
evidence; a name can be checked. What is in the frame is perceptual and is asked of the pixels;
whether that thing makes this sound is world knowledge and is asked as plain text, so a loosely
related object cannot count as the source without a table of which objects make which sounds.

\textbf{The action, not the object.} A baby in its mother's arms was named as the source of the
crying and silenced; a deaf viewer looking at it learns nothing about the sound. A second question
therefore asks whether the thing can be seen \emph{making the sound now}. Three signals vote
(open naming, the world-knowledge check, an open description of the frames); every two-way question
is asked in both option orderings with agreement required, because the 7B model prefers a position
(it answered ``(b)'' in 22 of 23 calls whichever option (b) was). Visibility is judged per 5\,s
stretch, but one continuous sound gets one continuous picture even if its source enters the frame
midway.

\textbf{Speech as gate context.} For each sound with speech within three seconds, one text-only
forced choice asks whether the people on the soundtrack are reacting to it. A yes puts the sound
first in line when the panel is full and can rescue a sound just under the bar; it never reaches a
picture and never overrides visibility. It is not asked for fewer than four transcribed words, since
Whisper transcribes non-speech as phonetic junk. On the 100-clip run it was asked 77 times and
answered yes twice.

\textbf{The frames may choose between the detector's own candidates.} When two labels share both
boundaries within 0.6\,s and are not ancestor and descendant (one sound with two names, from the same
windows), the VLM is shown the frames and asked which it is, with ``cannot tell'' keeping both. The
audio proposes, the video picks, and nothing is added that the audio did not hear.

\subsection{Stage 5: one picture per source}
% Ontology dedup (AudioSet ancestor rule; family vs member by interval subtraction; similarity
% merges need time overlap) -- three attempts recorded (notes sec:dedup). Disambiguation among the
% detector's own candidates from frames (sec:disamb).
The detector emits label families (\emph{Laughter, Snicker, Chuckle}) and a clip once produced
three near-identical pictures of people laughing. Three general mechanisms were tried. Label
embeddings fail because single words embed too tightly (\emph{Dog}/\emph{Cat} 0.90 above
\emph{Laughter}/\emph{Snicker} 0.80). Asking the VLM whether two depictions show one thing fails
because it answers the question literally: ``different'' for 90\% of pairs, including an owl on a
branch against an owl's hoot. A similarity threshold fails because it must be re-tuned whenever a
prompt elsewhere changes, which happened three times. What works is the detector's own taxonomy: the
AudioSet ontology (CC BY 4.0), vendored as child$\rightarrow$parent edges, and the rule that one
label must be an \emph{ancestor} of the other -- a shared parent is not enough, since \emph{Animal}
would swallow \emph{Dog} with \emph{Sheep}. The rule is 14/14 on every pair the demos produced,
with no threshold. A family label explains a member only where they overlap in time (interval
subtraction), so an all-clip \emph{Bird} does not swallow a five-second \emph{Owl}. Depiction
similarity survives as a secondary signal at 0.80, and only for pairs that overlap in time.

\subsection{Stage 6: what the picture shows}
% Event-framed depiction (2-5 plain words, no place, no example sentences), forced-choice
% validation asked twice; FLUX over PixArt; panel with dwell, bursts, three rows.
\textbf{Depiction.} For each sound the VLM writes a two-to-five word event phrase (``glass
shattering'', ``audience clapping''), built from the detector's label, the sound's kind as seen in
the frames and a \emph{place} of at most four words asked for separately. A place replaced the
one-sentence scene description after that sentence's objects were glued onto every sound: an
\emph{Air horn} became ``air horn blaring from silver van'' and rendered as a picture of a van. The
transcript never reaches the prompt: when it did, a \emph{Hiccup} became ``Hiccup on the phone, slow
down, how many''. No example phrases are given; the prompt is built from the detector, the audio and
the video only.

\textbf{Validation by choice.} A yes/no check approved ``man on motorcycle drives past silver van''
as a picture of \emph{Laughter}. A comparative check (is the phrase closer to the label than the
scene sentence was?) was right on eight cases and 11/17 on seventeen, because it measured phrase
length. The working check asks the VLM to \emph{choose}: given the phrase and the other sounds
detected in the same clip plus ``none of them'', which sound does it show, asked in both orderings.

\textbf{Rendering and panel.} FLUX.1-schnell renders the phrase at 768\,px on a plain white
background (chosen over PixArt-$\Sigma$ on twelve real prompts). The panel is divided by
simultaneity, not inventory: rows are the largest number of sounds heard at once (at most three),
a picture is held at least 1.5\,s, bursts closer than 0.8\,s are one appearance, and a cell is empty
when its sound is absent. The first design parked every sound's picture beside the video for the
whole clip, dimmed when silent; a panel that always looks the same carries no information, and a
picture on screen while its sound is absent makes a claim a deaf viewer cannot check.

\subsection{Design process}\label{sec:process}
% Knobs set once on the dev split; failure catalogue (18 entries) with the >=3-cases rule;
% demos illustrate only. docs/plan_robustness.md.
Every earlier fix had been made on the clip in front of us, and each knob had been recalibrated
by the next clip. Since 2026-09-13 the rules are: thresholds are set once on the development split;
every failure seen in a demo is logged in a failure catalogue with clip, stage and confidence; a
design change needs three entries of one pattern or a metric move on the development split; demos
illustrate and never tune; and every change is general and re-measured. The catalogue holds 18
entries, seven fixed as general bugs, one decided (the place veto, off after two right and two wrong
in four uses), the rest open at one of three.

\section{Benchmark}\label{sec:benchmark}
% 274 clips (proposal named 300; usable yield 15-20%), four categories: unseen_ambient, mixed,
% seen_ambient, no_ambient. One annotator; blind re-adjudication kappa = 0.60 (notes
% sec:readjudication). First 209 tags predate any model filtering. Main runs use 100 clips,
% 25 per category.
\textbf{Clips and labels.} 274 clips were hand-labelled into four scenarios:
\emph{unseen\_ambient} (an ambient sound whose source is not on screen), \emph{mixed} (some
sources visible, some not), \emph{seen\_ambient} (every ambient sound's source is on screen) and
\emph{no\_ambient} (speech or music only). The central labelling rule is the redundancy test used
by professional captioners (DCMP): not ``is the physical source in frame?'' but ``does the picture
already tell the viewer that this sound is happening?'' -- wind roar over a rider filmed on a
moving motorcycle is \emph{seen}, and so are waves when the sea fills the frame. The main runs use
100 clips, 25 per scenario, so that abstention is scored on as many clips as augmentation.

\textbf{Yield.} The proposal named 300 clips. Four independent sourcing strategies (keyword
scraping, AudioSet temporally-strong segments, curated film scenes, UnAV-100 overlap moments) and a
three-stage automatic filter all landed in the same 15--20\% band of usable positives, and yield
fell as filtering grew. Yet 36\% of clips that contain ambient sound at all are unseen or mixed:
the phenomenon is not rare, the acquisition funnel discards it. The first 209 tags predate any
model-based filtering and are the unbiased sample.

\textbf{Label stability.} Sixty tagged clips were replayed shuffled with the label hidden: self-agreement
78\%, Cohen's $\kappa = 0.60$. The instability is in the positive class (\emph{unseen\_ambient}
reproduced 12/20 against \emph{seen\_ambient} 19/20), which is the class the gate is judged on.

\textbf{Tuning on the test set, checked.} The labelled clips were split 50/50 stratified by tag,
every threshold swept on one half and frozen on the other, for five seeds: the held-out half scored
higher than the sweep half in four of five, and the sweep chose the same corner of the grid each
time. There is no optimism to remove, because the thresholds are not doing the work; the gate is
limited by what a vision--language model can answer, not by its configuration.

\section{Evaluation protocol}\label{sec:eval}
% Proposal 6.1: VLM describes the augmented scene -> LLM writes a reference -> independent judge
% scores 0-4. Abstention scoring (notes sec:abstain); reference must be able to say "nothing is
% missing" (sec:reference).
\subsection{Three references}
\begin{description}
  \item[model-derived (\tagref{v2}, \tagref{v3})] as specified in the proposal: written by the
        system's own VLM from the detector's own events. Found circular on 2026-09-14 (review): it
        rewards whichever system repeats the detector.
  \item[human-grounded (\tagref{v2\_grounded}, \tagref{v3\_grounded})] the same reference corrected
        by the annotator's label; only computable on labelled clips; raises the abstaining system
        more than the baselines by arithmetic (notes \S{}Main Result).
  \item[independent (\tagref{v2\_indep}, \tagref{v3\_indep})] built by models that never see the
        system's output: Qwen2-Audio listens to the Demucs no-vocals stem and to the raw mix in
        10 s chunks (the union, speech tags dropped; the stem alone was empty on a third of the
        clips because Demucs pulls tonal sounds into the vocals stem); each claim is verified by
        CLAP, kept if this clip's audio ranks in the top 20 of the 100 benchmark clips for the
        claim's text with positive cosine (a mean $+2\sigma$ bar over text decoys, the first form,
        left 58 of 100 references empty against 25 clips tagged \emph{no\_ambient}; both are
        reported); Idefics3-8B looks at the frames for what is on screen; Phi-3.5-mini writes the
        reference (Llama-3.1-8B is gated and was not accessible to the account); a MiniLM sentinel
        ($\tau = 0.50$, uncalibrated) rejects sentences unsupported by the verified list. This is
        the corrected primary.
\end{description}

\subsection{Baselines}
Blind audio-to-image (depict everything heard, no gate) and audio captioning (text instead of a
picture); video reasoning off for both.

\subsection{Statistics}
Per-clip paired win/tie/loss, bootstrap 95\% CI on the mean difference (5000 resamples), and an
oracle-gate bound: the human tag as the gate, the best system's output where a picture is due
(\texttt{scripts/paired\_stats.py}).

Under every reference the blind baseline scores at least as high as the gated system on
\tagref{v2}; the interval excludes zero each time. The circular reference was not what held the
gate back. The oracle row is the ceiling any gate could reach on this benchmark with the best
system's output where a picture is due: 2.88--3.19, i.e.\ a perfect gate would beat the blind
baseline by at most $+0.05$ under the human-grounded reference and not at all under the others.

\textbf{The independent reference is not neutral either.} Its silence decision (``nothing beyond
the picture'') agrees with the human tag on 55 of 100 clips -- chance. The list-and-match
visibility step (Idefics3 asked for a list of sound-capable things, one word back per clip;
MiniLM cosine to each verified sound) calls almost every sound unseen, so on seen-ambient clips
the reference says the viewer would miss the sound and the blind baseline is rewarded for
showing it (seen\_ambient: blind 3.08, proposed 2.48). A per-sound question to the same VLM
(``is the thing producing \emph{X} visible? answer \texttt{visible: <name>} or \texttt{not
visible}'') was tried next and scored the same 55\%, because the rule ``silent only if every
verified claim is visible'' fails whenever one of five to eight audio-LM claims (\emph{video
game sound}, \emph{wind noise (microphone)}, \emph{clock ticking}) is not; that variant was
not judged (a 70\% agreement bar gates the judge pass). The reference's remaining weakness is
therefore the same question the system itself finds hardest -- whether a sound's source is on
screen -- and the honest reading is that no automatic reference built here can score the gate
decision; the human-grounded reference remains the only one that can, with the asymmetry
already stated.

\subsection{Onset timing}
% benchmark/eval_dcase_onset.json (job 28942869, 2026-09-14): 255 DCASE gold events with an onset
% 0.3-4.0 s into the 5 s clip, 9 classes mapped to AudioSet families; one BEATs pass, three
% stampings. Hand-labelled check: scripts/timing_compare.py, 7 onsets on 3 clips.
Against seven hand-labelled onsets on three clips the plain stamp was late by $+0.9$\,s on average
and the shipping method by $+0.3$\,s; that is an anecdote, so the same question was put to DCASE
2025 Task 3, whose events carry a start frame at 100\,ms. From 255 gold events (nine non-speech
classes, onset inside the clip) one BEATs pass was stamped three ways; an event counts as matched
when a detection in the same AudioSet family starts within 1.5\,s of it. Every error below is
measured on the matched events only -- 66 of 255 (26\%) at the display bar -- so the table
describes where a picture is stamped when a sound is detected, not how often it is.

\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
stamping & matched & mean signed & MAE & $\leq 0.25$\,s & $\leq 0.5$\,s \\
\midrule
sliding-window stamp (before 09-14) & 50 & $+0.29$ & 0.43 & 44\% & 68\% \\
+ hysteresis                         & 66 & $+0.22$ & 0.35 & 58\% & 74\% \\
+ hysteresis + occlusion (shipping)  & 66 & $+0.16$ & 0.35 & 45\% & 80\% \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}

Hysteresis is the gain: on the 50 events both methods match, MAE falls from 0.43 to 0.31\,s, and it
matches sixteen more events, because a sound that reaches the bar late is now traced back to where
it began. Occlusion adds nothing on average: on the 65 events both match it is better on 25 and
worse on 39 with an identical MAE of 0.34\,s (paired bootstrap difference $0.00$, 95\% CI
$[-0.11, +0.10]$). It trades a smaller late bias and more onsets within half a second for fewer
within a quarter-second; on knocks, where hysteresis alone is within 0.08\,s, it moves the onset
$+0.26$\,s late. The seven-clip number therefore overstated the method, and the honest statement is
that the shipping onset is within half a second for four detected events in five. Detector
recall on these five-second clips is the larger limit: laughter, footsteps and domestic sounds
rarely reach 0.35 on BEATs. Whether to keep occlusion is a
development-split decision left open (\S\ref{sec:process}); it does not alter detection or gating,
only where a picture is stamped.
