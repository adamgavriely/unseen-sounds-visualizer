MSc final project · Adam Gavriely · Bar-Ilan · status 1 Oct 2026

Seeing Off-Screen Sound

A deaf or hard-of-hearing viewer misses sounds they cannot hear and cannot see the source of. The system finds those sounds in a video and shows a small picture of each one beside it. No model was trained; existing models are chained.

In one line: on the 88 TEST clips the final system finds 22 of 65 needed sounds and shows 26 wrong pictures. Its viewer cost is 2.55 against 2.96 for showing nothing and 2.91 for the old pipeline. Most remaining errors are sounds no model hears clearly, or cases where the models disagree at about chance.

1 · The task

Draw a sound only when the viewer can neither hear it nor see where it comes from

needed Heard, not seen

A siren is heard, no ambulance on screen. A picture of an ambulance appears.

not needed Heard and seen

A waterfall is on screen. A picture of it adds nothing, so nothing is drawn.

not in scope Speech and music

Speech goes to subtitles. Only non-speech sounds are drawn.

The hard part is two questions at once: which sound is it and when does it start (audio), and is its source on screen (vision). A look-alike is the classic trap: a bird is on screen but a different bird sings.

Clips: city walks, nature, AudioSet events, film scenes and web clips. 109 clips were labelled by me; 50 newer clips by a human tagger in 3 batches (batch 3 with the AudioSet labels visible, disclosed). A sound is needed when it is non-speech and its source is not visible during the sound.

How a picture is scored

OutcomeRule
hitA picture of the same sound family that starts between 0.5 s before and 1.0 s after the needed sound starts. One hit per needed sound.
missA needed sound with no hit.
wrong · visibleA picture of a sound whose source is on screen.
wrong · crossThe wrong sound type at that moment, or the right type at the wrong time, or a second picture of a sound already drawn.
wrong · phantomNothing is there.
Viewer cost per clip = (4 × misses + 2 × wrong pictures) ÷ clips. Lower is better. One miss weighs as much as two wrong pictures. Show nothing scores 4 × needed ÷ clips. A second sweep counts an on-screen wrong picture as 0, 1 or 2, since a viewer minds a picture of something they can see less than a false one.

How a change is accepted

  • Written down first. Every change has its rule and pass bar committed before any number (docs/prereg_round13_detector_push.md, 74 rounds).
  • Held-out guard. Audio rules are first checked on the 415 AudioSet clips; a fail there stops the round before DEV.
  • DEV decides. Pass = no needed hit lost, cost lower, and at most 2 extra wrong per hit gained. Or: lose at most 3 hits, but remove at least 3 wrong pictures per hit lost, and cost still falls.
  • TEST reports. A change that wins DEV ships; TEST is then read once and reported. No setting is picked from TEST.
  • General rules only. No rule may name a clip or a video-specific case.

Pictures (unchanged since 28 Sept)

Image model

Qwen-Image-2512. In a blind 1.5-second glance test I recognised 26 of 54 pictures, against 14 of 54 for the older FLUX model (significant; one rater).

Picture check

The vision model must pick the right object from shuffled choices, or the picture is redrawn (up to 5 tries, then a word card). Caught 7/7 known bad and 33/33 planted wrong pictures; wrongly rejected 3 of 68 good ones.

Scores ignore the picture

A hit asks only "right sound, right time?". The picture model changes what the viewer sees, not the scores above.

6 · Why we cannot simply do better

Three findings that explain the remaining errors

1 · Models disagree at chance

On the 415 held-out clips, a detected sound that no listener names is right 22 % of the time, one listener 37 %, both 62 %. The remaining errors sit where the models disagree, so any rule that keeps more sounds also keeps more wrong ones.

2 · Seeing a thing ≠ seeing the source

Every vision variant tried (pointing, cropping, motion-sound sync, asking "is it acting now?") moves along one line: silence more on-screen sounds and you lose needed ones about one for one.

3 · Gold lists only salient sounds

In my blind re-rating of 41 pictures with hidden controls, about 1 in 6 "wrong" pictures was a real, wanted off-screen sound the gold does not list. Costs are a little pessimistic for every system alike.
