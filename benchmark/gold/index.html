2 · What a hearing viewer gets that a deaf viewer misses

Pre-filled with the sentence the system's own models wrote (corrected by your earlier clip label). Rewrite it in your own words where it is wrong or vague; keep exactly nothing beyond the picture if every noticeable sound's source is on screen.

Guideline (ten lines)

  1. Watch with sound once. Write down every sound a hearing viewer would notice. Speech and music never count as sounds here.
  2. The rule is the professional captioners' rule: a sound needs a picture only if the video does not already show that it is happening.
  3. "Visible" means seen making the sound now: a barking dog on screen is visible; a baby held quietly in arms is not visibly crying; an alarm box on a wall is not visibly ringing.
  4. Wind roaring over a rider filmed on a moving motorcycle is visible (the rider says it). Waves when the sea fills the frame are visible.
  5. A siren from behind the camera, a dog in the next room, glass breaking off screen: not visible → a picture is due.
  6. If the source is visible for part of the sound only, tick "visible" only if it is visible for most of the sound; add a note.
  7. "Covered by speech/music": you can hear the sound only with effort because talking or a soundtrack sits on top of it.
  8. Start/end: rough is fine (half a second). Use the video's time; [ and ] stamp the current time.
  9. Everything is pre-filled from the system under test — sounds, times, visibility, the sentence, the picture-due box. That saves typing but can anchor you: delete what you do not hear, add what it missed, flip what is wrong. Your ear and eye decide; the thesis discloses the pre-fill.
  10. The sentence: write what the sound adds for a hearing viewer, in plain words. If nothing is missing, use the "nothing" button.

Time: about 2–3 minutes per clip. Export your JSON when you stop (the page also keeps a copy in this browser).
